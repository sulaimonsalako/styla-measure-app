Sizing, measured

A 42″ bust is a size 12 at Eloquii and a 16 at Gap

Same body. Same measurement. Four different numbers on the label. We read six brands' own published size charts — and found out why your size can go down when you shop plus-size.

By Sulaimon Salako, CEO of Styla · 27 July 2026 · 6 min read · How we source our data

If you've ever measured yourself properly, checked a brand's size chart, ordered the size it told you to, and had it arrive completely wrong — this is for you. And before we go further: that wasn't you doing it badly.

The standard advice is measure, check the chart, order that size. Millions of women have followed it exactly and still ended up with a returns label. The reason is that a size chart tells you what one brand means by a number. It tells you nothing about what a different brand means by the same number. The gap between them is far bigger than most people realise.

So we stopped theorising and pulled the actual numbers.

The answer: anywhere from a 12 to a 16

We took the published women's tops charts from six brands and asked one question: if a woman has a 42-inch bust, what size is she at each?

Why the number goes down at plus-size brands

This surprises people, because the assumption runs the other way. If you're shopping plus-size, you expect bigger numbers.

But plus-size brands build their charts on a different starting point. A straight-size brand's grid runs from very small to moderately large, so by the time it reaches a 42-inch bust it's near the top of its range — a 16, an XL, the end of the rail. A plus-size brand's grid is built for that body from the start, so 42 inches sits in the lower-middle. Eloquii's chart keeps going to a 69-inch bust. At that scale, 42 is a 12.

Same body. One brand treats it as an edge case, the other as the middle of the room. The number is telling you about the brand's assumptions, not about you.

Nearly five inches of difference behind one identical number. Five inches is not a rounding error — it's the difference between a shirt that closes and one that doesn't.

And sometimes there is genuinely no size for you

"I'm between sizes" is usually said as if it's a fact about your body. Often it's a fact about the chart. Grids step, and the steps leave holes:

If your bust is 44 inches, Birdy Grey does not make a size for you. Not slightly off — no size at all. Meanwhile Lane Bryant covers 42 inches to 68 inches without a single gap. The body that "isn't a size" at one brand is unremarkably mid-range at another.

You were never between sizes. The brand just didn't make one for you.

What to do with this

Your size isn't a property of your body. It's a property of the pairing — your measurements and that brand's chart. "I'm a 14" isn't a fact. "I'm a 14 at Old Navy and a 12 at Eloquii" is.

If you're near the top of a straight-size range, look across the line. The number will likely be lower, the fit better, and the grid won't run out from under you.

Check the chart's edges, not its middle. That one check explains an enormous number of returns.
